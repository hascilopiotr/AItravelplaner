# How to structure a single day

- **Pacing:** follow the visitor's pace setting (number of stops per day and the
  minimum buffer between stops). Within that range, fewer well-chosen stops beat a
  long list — the visitor should never feel rushed.
- **Slack between stops:** the buffer is for orientation, photos, toilets or a slower
  walk. Round arrival times to 5 minutes (10:05, not 10:03). Free time at the end of
  the day is fine.
- **Generous visit times:** use each place's `duration_min` as a minimum, not a
  target — big museums easily deserve 2 hours, meals 60–90 minutes.
- **Compact route:** pick one `area` for the day (plus its direct neighbours) and walk
  through it in one direction, like a loop or a line — never zig-zag across the city
  or come back to an area you already left. Prefer walking distances (<1.5 km) between
  consecutive stops.
- **Start:** begin in the day's area at the visitor's start time (not before).
- **Meals:**
  - Include lunch between 12:00 and 14:30 if the day covers that window.
  - Include dinner between 18:00 and 20:30 if the day covers that window.
  - Pick restaurants and cafés in the same area as the stops around them.
  - A café is a good short break mid-morning or mid-afternoon, not a meal replacement.
- **Variety:** alternate types of stops — e.g. museum → park → café → gallery.
  Avoid two long museums (90 min+) back to back.
- **Indoor/outdoor mix:** balance indoor and outdoor stops; in the cold season or
  late in the day, prefer indoor stops for the later part of the day.
- **End:** finish at or before the visitor's end time, ideally with a relaxed stop
  (dinner, bar, viewpoint, café).
